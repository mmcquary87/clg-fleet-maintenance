2MY OWN DIRECTION — THE DOWNTIME CLOCK
THE ARGUMENT
A work order is not the thing you care about. A tractor that isn't earning is the thing you care about.
So the unit is the object, not the ticketEvery screen is a list of units and how long each has been idle. Work orders are what's attached to a unit — never the top-level noun. A manager never has to translate "WO-2914" back into "3303 is stuck in Pensacola."
Status names the blocker, not the stage"In progress" tells you nothing. Lanes are Waiting on you, Waiting on a vendor, Waiting on parts, In the bay — every unit sits under the name of the thing that has to happen and the person who has to do it. The board triages itself.
Urgency is money and time, not a colorSeverity chips get gamed — everything becomes P1. A live clock and a dollar figure can't be argued with. Sorting is by cost of waiting, so the expensive problem floats without anyone claiming it.
This is the opposite bet from turn 1, which organized around the work order and the approval inbox. Both are defensible. This one is more opinionated and will feel wrong if your managers think in tickets rather than trucks.

1WORK ORDER PLATFORM — FIRST DIRECTIONS
WHAT I ASSUMED
Urgency model (I picked)
Three levels — Unit Down, Urgent, Routine. "Down" is a hard binary the whole queue sorts on; priority numbers (P1–P4) read as jargon to dispatch and get gamed.
Approval gate (I picked)
Dollar threshold: estimates at or under $500 auto-approve, above that a manager signs off. Matches the queue question you flagged — "what needs my approval" is the home screen.
Alvys is the system of record for the asset
Unit, trailer, VIN, driver, location, odometer, trip ID, domicile, last PM, warranty all arrive from the API and render read-only, with a sync stamp. Humans only type what Alvys can't know: the defect.
Vendor path is first-class, not an afterthought
Approved vendor list → estimate → PO/authorization number → invoice at close. In-house and vendor are two branches of one work order, not two products.
